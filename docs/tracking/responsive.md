# Responsive behavior

## What it is

How the tracking surfaces behave from a 320px phone to a 1920px monitor, and
how that is verified. §22 and §23 of the directive; M-82 built the harness and
found twelve defects with it.

## The lesson M-82 taught, first

Between M-73 and M-81, every accessibility scan ran in **jsdom with no
stylesheet attached**. Those scans passed. They were also incapable of
observing overflow, clipping, target size or contrast, because none of those
exist without CSS. Six modules of green proved considerably less than it
looked like.

The replacement runs real Chromium behind the real compiled stylesheets.
Twenty-two assertions failed on its first run and twelve genuine defects came
out — including `.ptable--cards`, a class that was supposed to collapse a
table into cards on narrow screens and never actually shrank it, producing
36–75px of horizontal overflow at 320px on the shipper list.

If you take one thing from this document: **a responsive test that does not
load the stylesheet is not a responsive test.**

## The breakpoints

Twelve widths are measured: 320, 360, 375, 390, 414, 480, 768, 820, 1024,
1280, 1440, 1920. That is a superset of §27's five named viewports (375×812,
390×844, 768×1024, 1024×768, 1440×900), and `tests/unit/section-27-coverage.
test.ts` asserts the five are still among the twelve.

Height is held at 900. The failure class the suite hunts — horizontal
overflow, clipped controls, unreadable tables — is a width phenomenon, and
varying height would multiply the runtime without adding a failure mode.

## What is asserted at every width

- **Nothing spills outside the viewport.** Not a form control, not an action,
  not a table cell.
- **No element is narrower than its own content**, which is how a button ends
  up with its label cut in half.
- **Every input renders at ≥16px.** iOS Safari zooms any field under 16px on
  focus, and the zoom scrolls the page sideways — an overflow bug that only
  exists on a real phone.
- **Tables are readable**: either they fit, or they genuinely become cards.
- **Maps are bounded**: never wider than their container, never taller than
  320px, never more than 60% of a phone's screen.
- **Nothing animates** under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.
- **No action available at 1920 is missing at 320.** The widest arrangement is
  the reference; a control that disappears on a phone is a feature a driver
  does not have.

## Accessibility (§23)

axe runs at 320, 768 and 1440 — the three arrangements the stylesheets
actually produce — with the tags `wcag2a`, `wcag2aa`, `wcag21a`, `wcag21aa`,
`wcag22aa`. Sampling three widths rather than twelve is deliberate: the rules
that vary with viewport (`target-size`, `color-contrast`) vary by arrangement,
not by pixel.

Beyond axe, ten assertions cover the things axe cannot:

- one `h1` per surface and no skipped heading level (this caught a real
  skipped level on `/track`, in all five locales — axe tags `heading-order`
  best-practice, not WCAG, so six scans had let it through);
- status carried by **text**, not colour alone;
- every filter control keyboard reachable and labelled;
- `:focus-visible` producing a visible outline;
- `aria-live` announcing states that change without navigation;
- error and empty states announced and readable;
- no rule revealing content on `:hover` without a `:focus` equivalent;
- the map's text alternative carrying the map's facts;
- document labels meaningful rather than file names alone.

## Contrast

The V4 palette is final and some of its values fail WCAG AA for *text*.
Decision Q7 allows dedicated companion tokens, so `--color-amber-aa`
(#8a5a00, 5.5:1 on paper) and `--color-slate-aa` (#5f6a71, 5.1:1) are used
where the original value would be applied to text. Decoration keeps the
original colour. The contrast report is in `docs/modules/M-59-responsive-a11y.md`.

## Fixtures vs live routes

Most surfaces are measured as **harness fixtures**: the real components,
rendered by the real code, written to `test-results/tracking-harness/` and
opened in Chromium. They are fixtures rather than live routes because the
routes need a Supabase session the e2e lane cannot mint, and seeding a
fabricated shipment would violate §30.

`/track`, `/driver/update/[token]` and `/es/track` **are** measured live. The
Spanish one is not decoration: §24's longest translated strings are the widest
failure case, and a notification link lands on that form in any locale.

The fixtures are regenerated by Playwright's `globalSetup` on every run. There
is no skip switch — stale fixtures are exactly the failure the harness exists
to prevent.

## The V4 media-query lesson

The prototype's responsive blocks sat **mid-file**, before the sections they
override. In CSS, later rules win, so every page overflowed at ≤480px. Moving
the blocks verbatim to the end of `v4.css` fixed every page at once. When
editing `v4.css`: media queries go last, and they stay last.

## Where the tests are

- `tests/e2e/tracking-responsive-a11y.spec.ts` — the twelve-width matrix, axe,
  and the ten manual assertions
- `tests/e2e/responsive.spec.ts` — the site-wide sweep
- `tests/e2e/axe.spec.ts` — the public pages
- `tests/unit/*-a11y.test.tsx` — the fixture emitters (they also assert
  structure, and they are what `globalSetup` runs)

## Extension points

A new tracking surface needs a fixture emitter and an entry in `FIXTURES`.
If it belongs to one of §27's six named surfaces, add its id to that surface
in `tests/support/section-27-catalogue.ts`, so deleting the fixture later
fails with the requirement it was covering.
